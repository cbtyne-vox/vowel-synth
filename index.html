fR1 strategy

Track H1 raises fR1 with the pitch once fo reaches it, the way a singer opens the vowel to keep whoop (Price). Track H2 keeps fR1 on 2fo, the yell or belt strategy.

Close timbre, for non-treble voices: from the start note up, fR1 sits between H1 and H2 so the voice stays out of open timbre. For [ɔ], [o], [e] and [ɛ], fR1 is centered between H1 and H2. [u] raises fR1 only as far as it needs to. [a] brings fR1 down a little and fR2 up a little. [i] keeps fR1 just above H1, or it starts to sound like [ɛ], and it starts tracking earlier, as soon as H1 gets close to fR1, so it never reaches whoop. [e] and [ɛ] also bring fR2 down a little as fR1 rises; the back vowels keep fR2 where it is. Below the start note it behaves like Fixed.

When tracking raises fR1, front vowels ([i], [e], [ɛ]) also bring fR2 down toward the middle of the vowel space, as singers do when they modify a vowel high in the range. Without it, fR2 stays where it is set and only moves up to stay clear of fR1.

About

Vowel Synth Lab was made by Chadley Ballantyne (Stetson University) for teaching acoustic registration in voice pedagogy. It follows the tradition of MADDE, Svante Granqvist's formant synthesizer, which many voice teachers have used for years to demonstrate harmonic and resonance interactions. The source is a Liljencrants-Fant glottal flow model (or, optionally, the closure transient alone), sent through five vocal-tract resonances. Built with Claude. Runs in Chrome or any current browser, with nothing to install.
